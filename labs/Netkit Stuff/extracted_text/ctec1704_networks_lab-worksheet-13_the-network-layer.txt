IPV4 Addressing

(IPv4) is the most commonly used version of the Internet Protocol (IP). Each device attached
to a network will have a unique IP address of the format 999.999.999.999. Part of the
address is the network identifier, and the remainder locates a specific host on that network.
The CIDR number (often seen as a /99 at the end of the IP address) tells a network device
where the divide between the network and host addresses falls.

Read some background about this topic here and here.
